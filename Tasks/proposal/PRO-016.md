# PRO-016 — A running grid bot shows its ladder, its summary line and where the price stands

**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Status:** Proposed
**Source:** The owner, 2026-10-19, running grid bots on a VPS: *"tui cần biết các grid cách nhau bao nhiêu % giá, mỗi grid sẽ TP được bao nhiêu"* ("I need to know how far apart the grids are in percent and how much each grid takes as profit"), and, after a list of seven candidate figures, *"chỉ cần làm 1, 2, 4. viết proposal"* ("only do 1, 2 and 4; write the proposal"). Those three are named in section 2. Asked for a proposal and not for code.
**Decision:** Pending. Nothing is accepted and no delivery task exists. Acceptance by the owner under `ONBOARDING.md` §7 is what turns this page into work.

## 1. Problem and evidence

Today the owner cannot answer three questions about a bot that is running: how far apart its grids are, how much one completed cycle earns at each grid, and where the price stands in the range. The figures exist in the application; they are not shown for a running bot.

Checked in the repository at `15379eb` (2026-10-19), by reading and by text search, not by running:

- `src/modules/bots/domain/grid/grid_derived.py` → `derive`, `GridDerived`, `GridProfit` already compute, from a bot's parameters, the exchange terms and the plan, the step between neighbouring levels as a fraction of the buy price (`step_fraction`), the net fraction after both legs pay the maker fee (`net_fraction`), the average net profit of one cycle in the quote asset (`cycle_profit_quote`), the value at each edge and the cycles needed to recover the loss at the lower edge. Arithmetic spacing gives a step that shrinks in percent towards the top; geometric gives the same percent at every grid (`grid_spacing.py`).
- The only caller of `derive` is `grid_evaluation.py`, which feeds the plan-time checks and advice texts (`grid_advice_texts.py`: "The thinnest grid step is …"). Those appear while a plan is edited and only when a check fires; no standing table exists, and nothing in `src/modules/bots/ui/bots_screen/` or `src/modules/bots/cli/` reads `GridDerived`.
- `bot list` / `bot show` (`src/modules/bots/cli/bot_status_reports.py`) print the parameters on request (`--params`, EPIC-038L) but no step, no per-grid profit and no price position. The Bots tab detail panel (`bot_facts.py`) shows earnings and inventory, none of these.
- What the saved run already holds (`src/modules/bots/domain/grid/grid_runtime.py` → `GridRuntime`, `RuntimeLevel`; `src/modules/bots/contracts/bot_progress.py` → `BotProgress`): the level prices, each level's state (`LevelState`) and resting order, the last price the bot heard (`mark_price`, `mark_price_at`), the completed cycle count, `start_price`. `RuntimeLevel.ended_at` records when a level's order ended, which may give a per-level cycle count; not read closely enough to rely on.
- The stop loss and take profit prices come from the parameters (`grid_params.py`: `off`, `price:<p>` or `percent:<n>` beyond the range edge). A Futures bot's liquidation price is watched by `GridRiskWatch` (`EPIC-039F`, `application/kinds/grid/watch/grid_risk_watch.py`) from the exchange's report, and `BotProgress` does not carry it.

Unknown, to be settled by whoever builds it: whether a read-only copy of the data root (every CLI read) holds the last price for a bot that has stopped or halted, and how old it is (`mark_price_at` says); whether the Futures liquidation price reaches the saved record or only the host's memory; whether a per-level cycle count can be derived from `ended_at` without a stored field.

## 2. Intended outcome and scope

**Phase 1, the subject of this proposal, three items the owner chose:**

1. **The ladder of a bot.** One row per grid, the buy level and the sell level above it: the buy price, the sell price, the step in percent, the net profit one completed cycle there takes after both fees (percent and quote), and what the level is doing now (waiting to buy, waiting to sell, empty) as the saved run records it. In the Bots tab detail panel as a table, and in `bot show` as a `--grids` section, in text, JSON and key=value like the other reports.
2. **A summary line above it.** Spacing model, smallest and largest step in percent, the average net profit per cycle, and the fee used (maker, both legs). One line a person reads at a glance, in the tab, in `bot list` or `bot show`, and in the plan.
3. **Where the price stands.** The last price the bot heard and its age; the percent distance to the lower and upper edge of the range; to the stop loss and the take profit when set; on Futures the liquidation price and the distance to it when the saved record has one, otherwise the line says it is not known.

Every figure is labelled with what it is: from the parameters ("designed"), from the saved run ("recorded"), or an estimate. A figure that needs a price the bot has not heard is shown as such and never as a zero (`grid_pnl.py` already follows this rule). The ladder table never claims profit per day or any promise about how often price will cross a level (`domain-truth-rule.md`, no unsupported claim; the backtest is where a rate belongs).

**Out of scope, on purpose:** earnings over time (profit per day, APR, cycles per day), which was candidate 3 and the owner did not choose; per-level heat (which levels fill most) and sleeping-bot detection (candidate 6); risk lines beyond the distances above (candidate 5); a comparison table across bots (candidate 7); any change to an order, a bot's behaviour, an alert or a stored field other than what the unknowns above force; Discord.

Success is observable: for a bot on the fake exchange, the ladder rows equal `derive`'s grids to the cent; the percent steps of an arithmetic and a geometric bot differ the way `grid_spacing.py` says; the distances equal the arithmetic on the saved last price; a stopped bot with no price shows "no price yet" and not zero; `bot show --grids` agrees with the tab; the figures use the application's formatter (`ui-presentation-rule.md` §9) and the table passes the conformance suite.

## 3. Options and trade-offs

| Option | Benefit | Cost and risk | Evidence or precedent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Keep the current approach | Nothing to build | The owner reads the ladder off the parameters by hand; the plan-time figures vanish once the bot runs | Today's behaviour |
| **A. Show the figures `derive` already computes, plus the price position, in the tab and in `bot show`** (this proposal) | Small: the arithmetic exists and is tested; one source for tab and CLI | A running bot's parameters can be edited (`bot edit`), so the "designed" table must come from the parameters in the saved record at read time, not a cached copy | `grid_derived.py`, `bot_facts.py` (pure text functions per figure), `bot_status_reports.py` |

Compute the ladder from the exchange's open orders instead of the saved run | Shows what is really resting | A second source that can disagree with the saved run; the app's rule is that the run's own account is never a safety input and the exchange is judged by the reconcile | `bot_progress.py` header, ADR D6 |

## 4. Recommendation

Option A, as one delivery task in two cuts of the same change:

1. **A pure read model in `bots`.** One function from a bot's saved record to a `GridLadderView` (rows, summary, price position) beside `grid_derived.py`, reusing `derive` and `GridRuntime`; no I/O, no exchange call, so a read-only copy and the tab call the same code. It does not duplicate any arithmetic.
2. **Two displayers.** The tab's detail panel table and the `bot show --grids` section (and the summary line in `bot list`), each only wording what the read model returns, by the kind-and-column rules of the workbench and `report_output.py`. The Futures liquidation line shows what the saved record holds; making the host save it is a separate small task if the unknown above says it is missing.

Dependencies and open questions that could change this: the three unknowns of section 1; the percent shown for geometric spacing is constant, so its column reads the same on every row, which is true and fine; for an arithmetic bot the bottom row is the widest in percent, and the summary line says so.

## 1. Problem and evidence

On 2026-10-03 the current tree was read by a survey of `src/modules/`, using file reads and
`grep`. Each claim below cites its file, and nothing here is inferred from task files. Paths that
do not start with `Docs/`, `Tasks/` or `src/` are under `src/modules/`.

### 1.1 A strategy is a setting of a desk, not a thing that runs

- **The strategy card lives on the desk.** Each desk embeds the strategy card
  (`trading/ui/dashboard/dev_board_widgets/strategy_card.py`). The card holds a strategy combo, a
  timeframe combo, % capital, leverage, a params button and Arm/Disarm buttons.
  `desk_view.py:100-103,135-152` places it next to Enable/Disable and Emergency Stop.
- **The traded symbol comes from the chart.** A strategy trades whatever symbol the chart shows
  when Arm is pressed: `desk_strategy.py:65` passes
  `get_active_symbol=lambda: chart.shown_symbol`. Changing the chart therefore changes the
  strategy's configuration, and nothing on the screen says so.
- **There is no lifecycle to watch.** `IStrategyArmingControl` offers `arm(config)`, `disarm()`
  and `saved_selection()` and nothing else (`trading/contracts/i_strategy_arming_control.py:30-45`).
  It has no pause, no resume, and no state beyond "armed with config X".
- **Orders cannot be attributed.** The strategy has no name, no running PnL of its own and no list
  of the orders it placed. Client order ids are `SEW-` followed by 12 hex characters
  (`trading/contracts/client_order_id.py:28-40`). They record neither the source nor the strategy,
  so a fill cannot be traced back to what placed it.
- **The Dev Board reaches the primary venue only.** It shows the same card, wired to that venue
  (`strategy/composition/port_bindings.py:157-160`).

### 1.2 One strategy per venue, one symbol per strategy, by structure

- **One session per venue.** `VenueStrategySessions` holds one `LiveStrategySession` per venue
  (`strategy/application/services/venue_strategy_sessions.py:29-45`). That session has a single
  config, engine and coordinator, and `arm()` replaces them
  (`strategy/application/services/live_strategy_session.py:91-93,160-179`).
- **One symbol and interval per session.** `dispatch_tick()` drops every tick that is not the
  armed symbol and interval (`live_strategy_session.py:198-222`).
- **One lease owner for every strategy.** The lease owner is the constant
  `STRATEGY_OWNER = "strategy"` (`strategy/contracts/strategy_owner.py:25`). Its docstring already
  anticipates a second instance with a different owner value.

### 1.3 What exists and what Grid can reuse

- **Symbol lease.** One owner per symbol. While a strategy holds a symbol, a manual order on it is
  refused with `SYMBOL_LEASED` (`trading/application/trading_session_state.py:238-273`;
  `execute_order/handler.py:141,207`).
- **Emergency Stop is per venue** (`emergency_stop/handler.py:110`). It disables trading, cancels
  every open order, and closes Futures positions or sells Spot holdings above the baseline.
  - It does **not** disarm the strategy and does not release the lease.
  - This is deliberate. The vocabulary entry for Symbol lease explains why: the position most needs
    its owner right after an Emergency Stop.
- **Order plumbing a Grid needs is already in place:**
  - limit orders with time in force (`EPIC-028O`);
  - the venue's price and quantity filters;
  - the minimum-notional pre-flight check;
  - order truth taken from the user data stream;
  - fake exchange routes for both venues;
  - a Spot Testnet venue.
- **The strategy catalogue already generates parameter forms.**
  - Typed parameters are declared through `ScriptInput`
    (`src/support/indicators/scripting/inputs.py:24-56`).
  - `StrategyCatalogService.params_form()` turns them into a form.
  - A Grid's parameters can follow the same form idiom.

### 1.4 What Grid needs and is missing

- **No Grid execution of any kind.** No bot entity or bot id exists, and `grep` finds no `grid`,
  `dca` or `bot_id` in `src/`. Grid bots were explicitly deferred (`EPIC-028/README.md:99`).
- **The backtest cannot simulate a resting order ladder.**
  - In a static run, strategy orders fill at the next candle
    (`backtesting/ui/logic/backtest_limitations_view.py:21-40`).
  - The only "ladder" in the code is the scale-out take-profit
    (`backtesting/contracts/broker_simulation_config.py:74`).
  - Fees are one flat rate with no maker/taker split (`broker_simulation_config.py:36-37`). A grid
    pays maker fees on its resting orders and a taker fee on its opening market buy.

### 1.5 The user's Grid reference (`grid_trading_phan_tich.docx`)

The report works one example through: BTC at 65,000, capital 10,000 USDT, a range of 60,000–70,000,
10 grids and a 0.1% fee. Its content, which this proposal takes as requirements:

- **Spacing.** Arithmetic spacing has `step = (U−L)/N`, and each grid nets `step/buy − 2×fee`.
  Geometric spacing has `ratio = (U/L)^(1/N)`, and each grid nets `ratio − 1 − 2×fee`.
  - For ranges narrower than about 20% the two give almost the same result.
  - For wide ranges, arithmetic leaves very thin margins on the upper grids, so geometric is
    preferred there.
- **Number of grids.** More grids mean more fills but less profit per grid.
  - Break-even requires `step% > 2×fee`.
  - The report recommends at least 0.5% per step, to leave room for slippage.
  - In the example, 77 or more grids lose money.
- **The levels and how to set them:**
  - The upper limit sits at resistance, the upper Bollinger Band, or by ATR.
  - The lower limit sits at support. The range should be about 2–4× the daily ATR(14).
  - Stop loss sits 3–8% below the lower limit.
  - Take profit sits 3–8% above the upper limit, or is a target on total profit.
  - On Futures, the stop loss must sit before the liquidation price by at least one ATR.
- **Start-up.** Half the capital buys the base asset to fill the sell levels above the current
  price; the other half rests as buy orders below it.
- **Behaviour at the edges:**
  - Below the lower limit the bot holds coin. In the example it is down 5.4%, against 7.7% for
    buy-and-hold. Recovering that loss takes about 40 grid cycles, so a grid is *not* a strategy
    that cannot lose.
  - Above the upper limit everything is sold, and the bot trails buy-and-hold: +2.3% against +7.7%.
- **The four synthetic scenarios.** These were run on 500 simulated price steps, not real data:

  | Scenario | Bot | Buy-and-hold |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | Ranging | +9.70% | +4.42% |
  | Uptrend | +4.43% | +115% |
  | Downtrend (stop loss) | −9.88% | −48.6% |
  | Crash then recovery (stop at the bottom) | −8.71% | +14.1% |

**Assessment of the report.** Its formulas and arithmetic check out; for example,
1,000/60,000 = 1.667% per grid. The report admits it has three limits, and each becomes a
requirement here:

1. Its results are synthetic.
2. It fills orders on touch.
3. It has no slippage.

The report's own conclusion is that a grid is a bet on a range-bound market. Its risk is a trend in
either direction, not a missing signal. This is why the stop loss, the take profit and a backtest
on real data come before live money.

## 2. Intended outcome and scope

**Outcome.** A top-level **Bots** tab is where all automated trading is created, started, watched,
stopped and deleted. Its first bot type is a **Grid bot** built on the report in §1.5. The desks
become manual trading only, with a read-only view of any bot running on their venue.

## 4. Recommendation

**Option D.**

### 4.1 The Bot entity (common to every kind)

- **Bot.** A named, persisted instance of `(kind, venue, symbol, config)`, with a lifecycle and an
  id. This is a new vocabulary entry, distinct from the `BOT-nnn` task-id prefix.
- **Bot kind.** A plug-in seam. Each kind supplies:
  - its parameters and the panel built from them;
  - its executor;
  - its chart overlay.

  The kinds are `grid` first, then `signal`, which wraps today's strategy engine, then `dca`.
- **Ownership.** The bot's id is its lease owner. Its client order ids carry a short bot tag. The
  direction is `SEW-{bot6}-{hex10}`, which uses 21 of Binance's 36 characters. A Grid needs this
  tag to recognise its own resting orders after a restart.
- **Lifecycle.** Draft → Running ⇄ Paused → Stopped.
  - An Emergency Stop on a venue **halts** every bot on that venue. The bot keeps its lease
    (§1.3), and only a person can resume it.
  - A takeover stops the bot and releases its lease. Its open position or holding becomes manual.
  - For a Grid, *Stopped* also cancels its resting orders. The user then chooses whether to keep
    the base asset or sell it, and the dialog says so before it acts.
- **Placement.** A new `bots` module owns the entity, the store and the lifecycle, and contributes
  the `bots` route.
  - `trading` stays the only module that places orders.
  - The `signal` kind will reach `strategy` through published ports, as
    `DECISION_2026-09-17_strategy_ui_contributes_rather_than_being_imported.md` requires.
  - The exact split is settled in the epic's decision record.

### 4.2 The Grid kind (from §1.5)

**Every input belongs to the user.** The Grid never fixes a parameter. It calculates from what the
user entered and returns a **verdict per check**: *OK*, *Warning* or *Refused*, each with its
reason and the numbers behind it. Only two things refuse, because each one makes the outcome
certain:

- `step% ≤ 2×fee`, where every cycle loses money;
- a capital per grid below the venue's minimum notional, which the exchange rejects.

Everything else from the report is a *warning* with an editable threshold:

- a step below 0.5%;
- a range outside 2–4× daily ATR(14);
- a stop loss or take profit outside 3–8%;
- the arithmetic spacing chosen on a range wider than about 20%.

- **Inputs:** lower and upper limit, number of grids, arithmetic or geometric spacing, total
  capital, stop loss and take profit (a price or a total-profit %), and fee rates.
- **Derived values, recomputed on every change:**
  - the step;
  - the net profit per grid at the bottom and at the top, after fees;
  - the capital per grid;
  - the smallest move that completes one cycle;
  - the opening base-asset purchase;
  - the account value at the lower limit and at the stop loss, against buy-and-hold;
  - the number of cycles needed to recover the loss at the lower limit.
- **Suggestions:** a range from daily ATR(14), and upper and lower limits from Bollinger Bands.
  These are offered values, which the user applies or ignores.
- **Execution:**
  - It reacts to fills from the user data stream, not to candles.
  - Each filled buy places the sell one level up, and each filled sell places the buy one level
    down.
  - The ladder state is persisted and reconciled at boot against the open orders that carry its
    tag.
  - A stop loss or take profit cancels the ladder and closes the position.
- **The bot's chart.** Each bot has its own chart: candles of its symbol plus its kind's overlay.
  - For a Grid, the overlay draws:
    - the upper and lower limits;
    - every level, coloured by its state (resting buy, resting sell, empty);
    - the stop loss and take profit;
    - a marker per fill;
    - the average cost;
    - while planning, the proposed levels and the ATR or Bollinger band behind a suggestion.
  - **It is the same chart and overlay in three places:** the planner preview, the backtest result
    and the running bot.
  - It reuses the existing chart, `ChartCard`. It follows the rule of
    `trading/ui/desk/desk_screen/strategy_overlay_coordinator.py:1-25`: one computation draws both
    backtest and live, so the two cannot disagree.
  - Fills already reach a chart per symbol through `DeskChart.record_fill`
    (`trading/ui/desk/desk_screen/desk_chart.py:15-17`).

### 4.3 Phases — a fast track for one Grid bot, then the rest

**Fast track.** The goal is one Grid bot trading on Spot Testnet as early as possible. F2a and F2b
run in parallel, and only F0 → F1 → F2b → F3 is on the critical path.

| Step | Delivers | Proves |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **F0 — Bot seams** | The Bot entity, id, kind seam, store, per-bot lease owner and tagged client order id. It is built for many bots, with a runtime cap of one. Today's arming is untouched. | The entity can hold a Grid and is ready for many |
| **F1 — Grid planner** | Inputs → derived values → a verdict per check (§4.2), plus the suggestions. Pure domain code, no orders. | The user's parameters are checked, never fixed |
| **F2a — Grid backtest** *(parallel)* | A ladder simulator on real candles. A historical-tick run decides the order of fills when a candle crosses several levels. Maker/taker fees, stop loss and take profit, and buy-and-hold as the comparison. The result is drawn by the bot's chart overlay. | What a configuration would have done on real data |
| **F2b — Live Grid bot and its tab** *(parallel)* | The Bots tab with the common shell, the Grid panel, the bot's chart (live and planner preview), the fill-driven ladder executor, boot reconciliation, Pause/Resume/Stop and Halted. The session limits are reviewed for ladders (§4.4). | A ladder runs and recovers on a real exchange API |
| **F3 — Testnet soak** | The bot runs on Spot Testnet across restarts and an Emergency Stop. The report notes what the ladder did, set against what F2a predicts for the same period. | The fast track's outcome: trading for real on Testnet |

**After the fast track.**

| Phase | Delivers |
| :--- | :--- |
| **P1 — Desks manual only** | The strategy card leaves both desks. The read-only bot badge, the lease refusal that names the bot, takeover, and `SPEC-010` written as "run a bot". |
| **P2 — Many bots** | The runtime cap is lifted to one bot per symbol, with a notional cap per bot and PnL per bot. |
| **P3 — Grid on Futures** | Leverage, the liquidation guard (stop loss at least one ATR before liquidation), and the Long, Short and Neutral modes |
| **P4 — Signal and DCA kinds** | The existing strategies as a `signal` kind, and DCA on the ladder executor |
| **Mainnet** | Through `EPIC-026`'s gates. Its Grid-relevant tasks are the mainnet venue, the caps and typed acknowledgement, the read-only first contact, the user data stream watchdog, resilient submission and alerting. A real-data backtest of the configuration is also required (§2.1). |

### 4.4 Dependencies and risks

- **A grid places many resting orders.** `max_orders_per_session` and `min_order_interval`
  (`trading_limits.py:42-61`) were written for one signal at a time. F2b must decide whether they
  apply per bot or per venue. Changing them is a configuration change under `ONBOARDING.md` §7.
- **Intrabar fill order.** A candle can cross several levels in both directions. F2a resolves the
  order from historical ticks where they exist and otherwise takes the conservative order. Each
  result states which rule it used.
- **Trend risk is the strategy's nature (§1.5).** The planner shows the downside before Start, and
  the stop loss and take profit are on by default.
- **`EPIC-026`'s operational stages (`PRO-005`) assume one strategy per venue.** If this proposal
  is accepted, its soak and monitoring tasks should name bots.

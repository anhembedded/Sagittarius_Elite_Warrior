# PRO-012 — A position drawn on the chart becomes a bracketed Futures order in one confirmation

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** Proposed
**Source:** The owner, 2026-10-15, with three TradingView screenshots of its "Short position" tool (entry, stop and target boxes on a 5-minute ETH chart, and its inputs and format dialogs): *"ở module trading, tui muốn tạo 1 tool vẽ như này, giống như trên tradingview, chỉ cần vẽ, và nhấn xác nhận là sẽ có lên trên binance … tui nhìn price action, nên tui cần cái tool đó để trading cho nhanh … đây là 1 hướng khác của bot"* (in the trading module, I want a drawing tool like TradingView's: draw, confirm, and it is on Binance; I trade Futures by price action on 5–15 minute charts, entering orders by hand is hard to manage, so I need the tool to trade fast; this is another direction besides the bots). Answers to the coordinator's questions, same day: entry is selectable, limit at the drawn price or market at the click; risk is selectable as fixed USDT or a percentage of the balance, or sized from a profit or loss amount; several positions per symbol are allowed; Cross margin.
**Decision:** Pending (owner)

## 1. Problem and evidence
- The trading module's desks already submit manual Futures orders: Long/Short becomes a side and `reduceOnly` pair against the freshly read position (`src/modules/trading/domain/policies/manual_order_intent.py`, one-way mode). Every order is typed field by field. There is no stop or target attached to an entry, and no sizing from risk.
- For discretionary trading on 5–15 minute charts, the time between seeing a setup and having a protected order on the exchange decides the trade. Sizing by hand from a stop distance is where mistakes are made.
- The bots taught the same lesson twice on 2026-10-15 (BUG-211, BUG-212): a plan must be judged with the exact quantities the exchange will receive before anything is sent.

## 2. Intended outcome and scope
The owner draws a long or short position on a trading desk chart: an entry line, a stop box and a target box, each draggable. A side panel shows, live while dragging:
- the quantity, from the chosen sizing mode: fixed USDT at risk, a percentage of the available balance, or a profit or loss amount at the target or stop;
- the risk and reward in USDT and in percent, and the reward-to-risk ratio **after taker fees on both legs and an allowance for stop slippage**, next to the raw ratio;
- the margin used, the leverage this implies, and an estimate of where the account would be liquidated under Cross margin given the other open positions;
- every blocking problem, judged with the exact quantities and filters that will be sent (step, minimum quantity, minimum notional, price band, margin available, stop beyond the liquidation estimate).

One confirmation sends the bracket: the entry (limit at the drawn price, or market at the click, chosen per order), a reduce-only stop and a reduce-only target for that entry's quantity. After it fills, the drawing becomes the live position: unrealised PnL, drag the stop or target to move it (cancel and replace), move the stop to break-even, close at market.

Constraints:
- **Never a position without a stop on the exchange.** If the stop cannot be placed, the entry is closed or cancelled at once and the owner is told.
- **Several entries per symbol in one-way mode.** Binance nets them into one position, so each entry's stop and target carry that entry's quantity with `reduceOnly`, never `closePosition`, which would close every entry at the first target.
- Real-money confirmation as elsewhere (`App.RealMoney`); Futures Testnet first.
- Out of scope: hedge mode, trailing stops, partial targets, alerts (later phases or other proposals).

## 3. Options and trade-offs
| Option | Benefit | Cost and risk | Evidence or precedent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Keep the current approach | No work | Orders typed by hand, no attached protection, sizing by hand on a fast chart | The owner's request |
| Order ticket with risk sizing, no drawing | Smaller; the sizing and the bracket give most of the safety | Slower to use while reading price action; the owner asked for the chart | TradingView's own tool is drawing-first |
| Draw on the chart, one confirmation, bracketed entry (recommended) | Fast, visual, sized from risk; protection always attached | Chart interaction work; bracket lifecycle (fills, partial fills, cancelling the leftover leg, app closed while an entry rests) must be designed and tested on testnet | Chart overlays exist (the Grid levels, `grid_overlay.py`); order filters and real-money confirmation exist; Futures adapter from EPIC-039 |

## 4. Recommendation
Draw on the chart with one confirmation, in three phases:
1. **Futures Testnet MVP:** drawing, the live panel, the plan-time judgement and the bracket placement for a limit or market entry.
2. **Position management:** drag to modify, break-even, close, keyboard shortcuts, the position list per symbol.
3. **Mainnet**, after the owner has used the tool on testnet for an agreed period.

The judgement and the order sizing are one function used by both the panel and the placement, the BUG-212 lesson. The bracket lifecycle is the riskiest part and gets its own design note before code, covering which leg is placed when, who cancels the leftover leg when the other fills, and what happens if the app is closed while a limit entry is resting.

Open questions that could change the recommendation:
- Whether a stop placed before its entry fills behaves on Binance Futures as needed for a resting limit entry; verify on testnet.
- Whether the bots' Futures mainnet gate (EPIC-039 decision O7) should apply to manual orders. It was written for unattended bots; a bracket with a mandatory stop meets its intent, but the owner should say so.

## 5. Decision needed
The owner accepts, rejects or defers this proposal. Acceptance commits the project to an epic in the trading module with the three phases above, a design note for the bracket lifecycle first, and a reviewer session on every phase (real money). It also needs the owner's call on the O7 question for manual Futures orders before phase 3.
